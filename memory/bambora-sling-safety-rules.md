Bambora's wearing rules as Fatima states them when reviewing creator submissions
(given 2026-09-17, with reference photos):

- **The seat securely underneath the bottom is the non-negotiable one, for every
  size.** Fabric spread from the back of one knee to the other, weight on the seat.
- **The "M" position** (knees above bottom) applies to babies. For toddlers it is
  harder and *not as essential*, but the seat under the bottom still is. Don't
  write copy that demands a strict M for toddlers.
- **One hand supporting baby at all times**. She emphasises this repeatedly
  ("one hand on at all times***"). No hands-free shots.
- **Hardware:** buckle must be fully closed *over* the clip (not resting half on
  it) and the **safety loop must be in use**. She caught both failing on a real
  Trybe submission.
- Failure modes she names: seat not under the bottom, no M position, legs
  cutting off (fabric rolled narrow under the thighs).

**Approved product range: 10 to 50 lbs.** State it as a WEIGHT, never an age. Creators
have been inventing their own versions ("5 months to 5 years", "10 to 30 lbs", "up to
three years old") and all of those are wrong.

**"Hands free" is a retired claim.** Bambora no longer advertises the sling as hands free,
and one hand stays on baby at all times. Roughly a third of the pre-guidelines top
performers were built on that claim, so expect creators to reach for it by habit. The
approved replacement angle is wrist and arm relief (there is an lp-wrist-pain landing page
for it).

**Weight rule and newborn questions (Fatima, 2026-09-23; answer these without asking her):** the sling is for babies **10 to 50 lbs**. A newborn under 10 lbs must not be in it. When a creator asks about newborns: tell them it fits from 10 lbs, so they wait until baby reaches 10 lbs. Until then they can film with someone else's baby in the 10 to 50 lb range (a friend's or sister's baby) or **use a doll** in place of the baby, or do no-baby formats (tester, rating, authority). Nursing angles are fine once the baby fits the range. A doll is acceptable as a stand-in; this overrides earlier doll notes in reviews. Cradling a newborn under 10 lbs is not OK.
- **Draft check before any send:** scan inspo and messages for "hospital bag", "from birth", "day one", "newborn in the sling" and similar. The sling starts at 10 lbs, so any idea implying use straight after birth must be reframed or removed (2026-09-23; a hospital bag idea reached Katherine Bodie before this was caught).

**Dolls are a last resort (Fatima, 2026-09-23, Jen Smith):** we want creators filming with their own kids. Only suggest a doll (or a friend's baby) when the creator has NO child in the 10 to 50 lb range (pregnant, newborn under 10 lbs, no kids). If a creator with a child in range asks about a doll or says their kid won't cooperate, reply neutrally and don't bring up the doll.
When a creator with a child in range asks about using a doll, the answer (her words, 2026-09-23): most of our winners use an actual kid instead of a doll, because viewers form a better connection with a real child. Never volunteer the doll to them.

**Cooking is OK (Fatima, 2026-09-26):** "I think it's okay to cook with the baby." Do not flag cooking or "I cook" lines in reviews or inspo caveats. One hand on baby still applies.

**Chewing straps is fine (Fatima, 2026-09-26):** a baby chewing or mouthing the straps/buckle is NOT a safety issue. Never flag it in reviews or inspo caveats. Only flag what her rules actually say; review-agent opinions beyond the checklist go to her as questions, never straight to creators.

**Stairs and everyday scenes are fine (Fatima, 2026-09-26):** stairs are OK with one hand on the baby (the other can be on the rail). Cooking is OK. The test for any scene is whether the baby is clearly supported. Do not flag stairs in reviews, inspo or scripts when one hand is on the baby.
