# Founder overstock ad: teardown and top-of-funnel translation

Source file: `m1129616012962989__XHcWMl1b88.mp4` (74s, 9:16). Analysed 2026-09-22 from the full transcript plus frames every 0.5s in the hook and every 2s in the body.

## What the ad is

One voiceover ("As the founder of Bambora...") laid over roughly 35 different creator clips, a new mom every 1 to 2 seconds, word-by-word captions. Script beats:

1. Founder plus "one of the best offers we've ever offered"
2. Reason: we overproduced, "much larger"
3. Who it should go to: mothers wrecking their backs, screaming toddler in one arm, dinner in the other
4. Designed by a mom
5. Four feature and relief pairs (one strap vs buckles, no 15-minute tutorial, weight distribution vs back, 10 to 50 lb vs buying a new carrier yearly)
6. Identity: "a mom who clearly has her life together", "the parent other people notice at the playground"
7. 100,000 mothers
8. Offer restated, "same exact sling, same exact quality"
9. Won't be repeated, "check below to see if your color is still available"

## Stage of awareness

Most aware, leaning product aware. Bottom of funnel. The first six words name the brand and the first sentence is about an offer, which only means something to a person who already knows what a Bambora is. The body re-teaches the product, so it also converts solution-aware people who wandered in, but the hook is written for the warm audience.

## The principle

A credible insider makes an admission against their own interest, and gives the reason.

A discount with no reason reads as "cheap product" or "fake urgency". A discount with a reason ("we overproduced, that's our mistake") reads as luck for the buyer. The admission is what makes every later claim believable. Everything else in the ad hangs off that.

## Why the hook worked

- Standing: "as the founder" means a person is talking, not a brand, and she has the right to know.
- Open loop: "best offer we've ever offered" makes the viewer ask why, and the ad pays it off inside five seconds.
- Admission: "much larger than we planned. Much larger." The repetition sounds like a human wincing, not copy.
- Visual: three different moms in three different prints inside the first four seconds. Product is worn in every frame, so it works with sound off, and the face count is social proof before the 100,000 number is ever said.

## Why the body worked

- One specific scene instead of a general pain ("screaming toddler with one arm while trying to make dinner with the other"), and the b-roll shows exactly that scene.
- Every feature is welded to the pain it removes and to a named enemy: buckles, the 15-minute wrap tutorial, the carrier you rebuy every year. No feature is left as a spec.
- Price justification before the price ("same price from 10 to 50 pounds").
- Identity payoff after the practical payoff. Back pain gets attention, "has her life together" gets the purchase.
- Risk reversal aimed at the one doubt a discount creates: "same exact sling, same exact quality".
- Scarcity that is believable because it follows from the reason (overstock is finite).
- CTA is a micro-step, "see if your color is still available". It is curiosity, not "buy now", and it implies colors are already going.

## Flags on the original

- The clip at "piece of fabric" shows a mom drinking from a mug with baby in the sling. That breaks check 2 (no hot drinks, one hand on baby).
- "Fits from newborn to toddlers" is an age framing next to the approved 10 to 50 lb weight.
- Caption typo: "We prduced".

## Translating to top of funnel for Trybe creators

Creators cannot be the founder and cannot carry an offer. So the principle gets re-cast:

| BOF original | TOF creator version |
|---|---|
| Insider = founder | Insider = her own life: her body, her receipts, her job, her second baby |
| Admission = we overproduced | Admission = I wasted money, I was wrong, I judged it, I ignored the pain |
| Reason-why = overstock | Reason-why = what she worked out about why it hurt or why the other gear failed |
| Opens on brand and offer | Opens on a symptom or scene. No brand, no product name in the first 3 seconds |
| Closes on scarcity | Closes soft. No price, no discount, no urgency |

### Ten hooks

1. **The receipts.** "I've spent over $300 on baby carriers and I use none of them. Here's the pile." Opens dumping the carriers on the bed. Each one gets a one-line reason it failed, which sets up the feature and relief pairs.
2. **The misdiagnosed symptom.** "I thought my wrist pain was from my phone. It was from my 24-pound daughter." Opens on her rubbing her wrist, then the hip hoist she does all day. Unaware audience, and it lands on the approved wrist and arm relief angle.
3. **The day job.** "I'm a [nurse / physio / daycare teacher] and the way most of us carry our toddlers makes me wince." Only for creators who really hold that job. This is the closest match to founder authority.
4. **The second-time mom.** "With my first I carried him on my hip until he was three and my back never forgave me. I'm doing one thing differently this time." Experience as standing, plus an open loop.
5. **The apology.** "I used to think slings were for a very specific type of mom. I was wrong and I owe her an apology." Admission plus identity, played for a smile.
6. **The math.** "My toddler asks for 'up' about 30 times a day. She's 28 pounds. That's 840 pounds a day on one arm." On-screen tally. Specific numbers do the job "much larger" did.
7. **The silent scene.** No voice. Text: "POV: it's 5:40pm and she's been on your hip since 3." One-armed laundry shuffle, then the cut to the fix. Film the before without the sling, and not at the stove.
8. **The thing nobody warns you about.** "Nobody tells you the newborn stage isn't the hard carrying stage. It's when they're 25 pounds and still want to be held." A reframe that creates the problem for an unaware viewer.
9. **The regret list.** "Three things I regret buying for my baby, and the one I'd buy twice." Sling comes last. Trashing her own purchases first is what earns the recommendation.
10. **The stranger.** "A mom stopped me at the park to ask what this was. Third time this week." Opens mid-walk, already wearing it. This is the ad's identity payoff ("the parent other people notice") moved to the front.

### Body template for all ten

1. 0 to 3s: hook, on a symptom or scene
2. 3 to 10s: show the scene, prove it is real
3. The "because": what she worked out
4. Around 40%: the sling appears as a discovery, not a pitch
5. Two or three feature and relief pairs, each against a named enemy (buckles, the wrap tutorial, rebuying every year)
6. One identity line
7. Soft close, no offer

Guardrails: weight range stated as 10 to 50 lbs, never an age. One hand on baby in every frame. No "hands free". No cooking, hot drinks or stairs while wearing. Buckle closed over the clip with safety loop.
