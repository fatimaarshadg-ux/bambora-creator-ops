## Two things that reframe the ranking

**1. Kia's ads were scaled hardest, not just best.** $14.3k on a single
creative is the largest spend on any creative on the platform — 14% of all
Bambora spend. Part of her #1 position is a budget decision, not her creative.

**2. Thumbstop says the opposite of sales.** Cambria 45–48% vs Kia 26–30%.
Kia's ads hook *worse* and convert *better*. That gap is the discount doing the
work. On creative merit — the thing you can actually reuse when nothing is on
sale — Cambria, Myrka (51.7%) and Jennifer (57.7% on one) are ahead.

## Caveats

- **Two ranking definitions.** The table above uses your definition — sale-heavy
  like Kia's. Ciara Burnett makes #6 on that basis because her sale line is only
  in the final 2 seconds. Exclude *any* sale mention and she drops out, Carissa
  moves to #6 and Danielle Corbin enters at #10. Everything else is unchanged.
- **164 creatives (13% of sales) are unclassified.** Concentrated in Jennifer
  Thomas (48 more), Cambria (36 more) and Cloe. Cambria's lead is far too large
  to be affected; the #10–14 band could shuffle.
- **Cloe and katherine** have only one classified creative each, both sale-heavy.
  Their true non-sale figure is somewhere between $0 and their untested remainder
  ($1.1k and $190 respectively).
- Trybe Analytics defaults to **Last 7 days** and has no all-time preset — the
  range has to be set by hand every session, which is why the "top creator"
  changes depending on when you look.
- Rankings use **Trybe attribution**. Meta reports $171K purchase value, ~1.9x
  Trybe's figure, so the two models disagree substantially.
