# Bambora Top Performer Briefs

Reference pack for creators. One entry per winning video: what it is, why it worked,
and what **not** to copy from it.

**Important:** every video here was made *before* the new safety guidelines. They all
earned their spot on performance, but several contain things we would now send back.
Each entry flags exactly what to leave behind. Copy the bones, not the mistakes.

> Safety flags below come from the full audio transcript plus sampled frames across each
> clip. Before re-sharing any of these publicly, give it one full watch.

---

## 1. Blue hoodie, "most underrated baby item"
`brand_CambriaR_04fe6a44.mov` · 18s · no dialogue, text overlay, music

A mum at home simply puts her baby in the sling and carries on. No script, no pitch, one
line of text on screen doing all the selling. Proof that the quietest format on this list
still works: our audience stops for something that looks like a real person's camera roll,
not a commercial.

**Replicate:** the silent demo plus a single strong text hook. Real home, real light.
**Leave behind:** ⚠️ baby's legs hang straight down and the seat isn't tucked under the
bottom in several shots. There's also a moment with a phone in one hand and no clear hand
on baby. Both would fail the checklist now.

## 2. The ring sling comparison (plus-size mama)
`m1497247221510231_Hi8HP49vJeE.mp4` · 88s · talking, product in hand

The most educational video in the set, and the most on-message. She explains why a
traditional ring sling is a faff (all that pleating, the ring, swapping shoulders), then
walks through our padded strap, the clips, the safety buckles, and how much room there is
to extend as a plus-size parent.

**Replicate:** honestly, most of it. She nails the seat explanation without being told to,
including knees up, weight on the bottom rather than the legs or back, and keeping baby
close enough to kiss. Length is no barrier when the information is genuinely useful.
**Leave behind:** nothing significant. This one is closest to the new guidelines already,
and the plus-size angle is underserved.

## 3. Disney, 8 seconds
`m714912408336584_Ekt7xwNrslI.mp4` · 8s · talking to camera while walking

The shortest video here and one of the strongest. Minnie ears, a toddler on the hip,
walking the park. The hook is a specific place and a specific ache: queueing all day with
a toddler who wants carrying.

**Replicate:** the specificity. Not "it's comfy" but "I was in lines all day at Disney."
A named situation beats a general benefit every time.
**Leave behind:** ⚠️ one hand is holding the phone for the selfie and the other isn't
clearly on baby. The toddler's legs also dangle with a shallow seat. Film this one with
someone else holding the camera, or prop the phone.

## 4. "POV: self care in peace"
`m1530759294736519_jZHWgVdPLhs.mp4` · 22s · lullaby, text overlay

Mum doing her makeup in the kitchen with her toddler in the sling, singing to her. The
"Velcro baby" line in the CTA is the audience's own language and it lands.
**Replicate:** the POV text hook, the mundane setting, and "Velcro baby" as a phrase.
**Leave behind:** 🚫 the entire premise. The video sells doing something with both hands
free, and the visuals deliver exactly that. Under the new guidelines this can't be
remade as-is. If you want the self-care angle, it has to be a one-handed activity with the
other hand staying on baby.

## 5. "Change their view"
`m1167838954929799_1yJEjUJIx0k.mp4` · 23s · no dialogue, text story, music

A little story told entirely in captions: a bored toddler staring at the floor, then up in
the sling seeing the world while mum gets on with the laundry. Clever structure, strong
emotional payoff, no talking at all.
**Replicate:** the problem-then-solution arc told in text over real footage. The toddler's
point of view is a fresh angle nobody else is using.
**Leave behind:** 🚫 the beat where she holds up both hands to camera, and the hands-free
laundry sequence. Same issue as above. The "they get the view" half of the story is still
completely usable.

## 6. The on-and-off demo
`m2865469623650524_dnhBm0vWDds.mp4` · 25s · talking, demonstrating

Shows how fast the sling goes on and comes off, one movement over the arm and done, versus
what she calls the big clunky carriers.
**Replicate:** the speed demo. Showing the thing being used beats describing it.
**Leave behind:** 🚫 she says outright that you can be hands free with it. That phrase is
retired. The ease-of-use point survives perfectly well without it: easy to put on is still
true and still sells.

**Replicate:** the anti-bulky positioning, the packs-down-small demo, and prints as a

cold. Seat is also shallow with legs hanging.

## 9. The granddad interview
`m1528424161733122_0iuUK54Q6wQ.mp4` · 40s · off-camera interview

Someone off-camera asks granddad why he prefers it, and he just talks. He can put it on
himself, he can make eye contact with his grandson, the baby can see him. The warmth is

**Replicate:** the interview format and the multi-generational angle. Handing the question

⚠️ Also an on-screen claim of "5 months to 5 years old", which is not our spec. The
approved wording is **"Great for babies and toddlers from 10 to 50 pounds."**

## 10. Newborn, safety first
`m1376014540638875_NBWr3_psn6A.mp4` · 41s · talking, hard captions

A mum in a car park with her seven-week-old, talking through how the sling takes the
weight while she supports the head, points out the safety feature, and explains that it
won't drop the baby if it comes unsnapped.
**Replicate:** this is the safety template. Without being asked, she says she keeps a hand
on baby for safety reasons, mentions supporting the head, and shows the safety feature.
Point creators here when they ask what "safe but still natural" sounds like.
**Leave behind:** nothing. The newborn use case plus visible safety awareness is exactly
the direction we want more of.

Just arrived in the mail and she's obsessed. The standout line is about the relief on her
wrist from holding the baby, which connects to a pain point we already have a landing page

**Replicate:** the wrist and arm ache angle, and her honesty. She caveats that you'll want

# What the winners have in common

**Dads and grandparents are a proven lane.** Four of fifteen, and they are among the most
distinctive things we have. Nobody else in this category is showing them.

**Five of fifteen have no dialogue at all.** Text overlay and music, real footage. If a
creator is nervous on camera, this is a complete format, not a fallback.

**Specific beats general.** Disney queues, a fairground at dusk, doing the laundry, a car
park with a seven-week-old. Named situations outperform "it's so comfy" every time.

**Anti-bulky is our strongest framing.** Four videos independently reached for some version
of "I don't want one of those big clunky carriers."

**Their words, not ours.** "Velcro baby" shows up twice, unprompted, in two of the best
performers. Worth putting into the weekly drop as language to use.

**Let the baby interrupt.** The moments where a baby waves, babbles or grabs the camera and
the parent just responds are the moments that make it not look like an ad.

# The approved range: 10 to 50 pounds

Bambora's approved range is **10 to 50 lbs**. Please use weight, never age. Three of the
videos above state it differently, and creators have been repeating those versions, so
here is the correct line for each:

| Video | What was said | Say this instead |
|---|---|---|
| 9 (granddad) | "Great for children from 5 months to 5 years old" | "Great for babies and toddlers from 10 to 50 pounds" |
| 13 (looks out) | "It goes from 10 to 30 pounds" | "It goes from 10 to 50 pounds" |
| 14 (Disney nap) | "Perfect for any age baby up to three years old" | "Perfect for babies and toddlers from 10 up to 50 pounds" |

If you are unsure how to phrase it in your own words, just message me and I will check it
before you film. 😊

# The retired line

**Hands free is gone.** It appears explicitly in videos 6 and 9, and it's the entire
premise of 4, 5 and 15. That's a third of our best-performing content built on a claim we
no longer make. Worth saying plainly in the weekly drop so creators don't reach for it out
of habit, because it clearly *did* work, and they will.

The replacement angle is the same benefit told honestly: the sling takes the weight off
your arms and wrists, so carrying stops hurting. That's what parents actually feel, and it
survives the guidelines intact.
