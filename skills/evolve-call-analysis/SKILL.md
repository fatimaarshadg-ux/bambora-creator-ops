# EVOLVE — Ad learnings call analysis

Turns the weekly ad learnings call transcript into a straightforward client
report: what was found, learned, and decided.

**This reads a transcript.** If the ask is "how is the account doing", that's an
account-data question and a different skill. This one only knows what was said
in the meeting.

## The workflow this reports on

- Strategy calls with clients develop creative concepts
- Ads are built from those concepts and run 4–7 days
- Each ad is reviewed in the weekly learnings call and classified **loser**,
  **winner**, or **super winner**
- Ads are tracked in ClickUp; only ads in the "learnings" phase get reviewed
- Discussion runs client by client
- Meta ad accounts use **CBO**, so whether an ad received significant spend is
  an important early indicator
- Metrics typically discussed: profitability (ROAS), hook rate (3-second views),
  hold rate (watch time), CTR, and other engagement metrics

## Report structure — per client

Each client gets a completely separate section. Never merge clients.

**Client name** as header, then:

**Executive summary** — 3–5 sentences. Opens *"This week, we reviewed [X] ads
and found that…"* Covers what was learned and decided for this client
specifically.

**Performance overview** — opens *"Here's what we saw in the account…"*.
Includes total ads reviewed and the distribution of winners / losers / super
winners.

**Individual ad analysis** — for each ad discussed:
- Ad name or identifier, if mentioned
- Metrics analysed (spend, profitability, key metrics)
- Classification: winner / loser / super winner
- Key insights about why it succeeded or failed
- Specific creative elements highlighted — hooks, visuals

**Key learnings and insights** — written as *"We noticed that…"* or *"We found
that…"*. Creative elements that consistently perform well or poorly for this
client, trends in their data, testing ideas and optimisations being recommended.

**Next steps and recommendations** — written as *"We're going to…"* or *"We'll…"*.
Specific tasks assigned to team members, new creative concepts being developed,
optimisation strategies planned.

Highlight exceptional performances and critical issues needing attention.

## Tone

- First-person plural — "we", "our team"
- Simple and straightforward; avoid formal or complex phrasing
- Insights and learnings over raw data
- Findings presented as discoveries and decisions the agency made
- No jargon; explain technical terms simply where they're needed
- Conversational
- **We create and run the ads, not the client.** This framing must hold
  throughout — it is the difference between a report that reads as ownership and
  one that reads as if the client did the work.

## Warnings

**Don't guess.** If an ad's status is unclear from the transcript, say so rather
than inferring it.

**Unattributed metrics get flagged.** If numbers are mentioned but not clearly
tied to a specific ad, note that in the client's summary rather than assigning
them.

**Extract, don't invent.** Report what's in the transcript. Don't add
recommendations that weren't discussed — the value of this report is that it's a
faithful record.

**Translate the casual talk.** Internal discussion contains jargon, shorthand,
and tangents. Convert it into simple client-facing language without losing the
substance.

**Report the decision reached, not the debate.** Team members may disagree about
a classification. Present where they landed.

## Note

The classification vocabulary — loser / winner / super winner — is the team's,
and the report should use it. But a classification is a decision the team made
in a meeting, not a metric. Where the transcript records *why* an ad was
classified the way it was, carry that reasoning across; a bare label tells the
client nothing they can act on.
