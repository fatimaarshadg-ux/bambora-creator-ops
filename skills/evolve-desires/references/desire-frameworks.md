# The desire frameworks

Every desire found in the mining gets traced to one of three permanent forces.
The point of the mapping is to answer *how long will this desire last*, which is
what Staying Power in the power ranking measures.

---

## 1. Mass Instincts — universal human drives

These never expire. A desire that traces here renews in every generation, which
makes it the safest foundation for a long-running campaign.

| Instinct | The drive | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Health** | Survival, safety, staying well | "I'm scared of what this is doing to me long-term" |
| **Sex** | Attractiveness, finding mates, feeling desired | "I don't recognise myself in photos any more" |
| **Status** | Importance, respect, superiority | "People ask me where I got it" |
| **Belonging** | Fitting in, friendship, avoiding rejection | "Everyone else seems to have figured this out" |
| **Control** | Power, predictability, being in charge | "I never know what I'm going to get" |
| **Comfort** | Ease, avoiding pain and work | "I don't have 40 minutes for this" |

Two notes that matter in practice.

**Status and Belonging pull in opposite directions.** Status is *above* the
group; Belonging is *inside* it. A quote that reads like both is usually
Belonging wearing Status's clothes — check whether the speaker wants to be seen
as better than their peers or accepted by them.

**Comfort is chronically underrated.** It reads as a weak motive and converts
like a strong one. Most "I gave up on it" quotes are Comfort, not Health.

---

## 2. Mass Technological Problems

Frustrations the category itself creates. These are the richest hunting ground
because they are specific, widely felt, and rarely addressed head-on by
competitors — every brand would rather talk about its benefits than about the
category's failures.

Each carries a distinct emotional residue, and that residue is what the copy
actually hooks:

| Problem | What it is | What it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|
| **Complexity** | Confusing interfaces, unclear instructions | Feeling stupid |
| **Overwhelm** | Too many choices, notifications, distractions | Anxiety |
| **Fragility** | Breaking when needed most | Frustration, betrayal |
| **Maintenance** | Constant updates, rising costs, repairs | Fatigue |
| **Incompatibility** | Won't work with what they already own | Feeling trapped |
| **Obsolescence** | Outdated almost immediately | Feeling cheated |

The residue is the useful half. "Complexity" is a product observation;
"it made me feel stupid" is an angle. Always carry the emotion forward into the
finding — a Mass Technological Problem logged without its residue is half a
finding.

---

## 3. Forces of Change

Forces that shift what people want and how they expect it delivered.

**Style trends** — what has become fashionable. Fast-moving, powerful while it
lasts, and the reason a winning angle can stop working without the product
changing at all.

**Mass education** — what society has collectively learned. Slower, more durable.
When a mechanism or ingredient crosses from specialist knowledge into common
knowledge, the desires attached to it change permanently.

**A desire traced to a Force of Change has a shelf life.** Rank it accordingly.
It can still be the right thing to run right now — it just cannot be the
foundation of a year of creative, and the document should say which it is.

---

## Applying the mapping

For each desire, record:

1. **The desire**, in the customer's words where possible
2. **The force it traces to**, and which of the three categories
3. **The emotional residue** — what it leaves the person feeling
4. **The evidence** — quotes with sources
5. **The shelf life** — permanent (Mass Instinct), category-bound (Mass
   Technological Problem, lasts as long as the category fails at it), or
   time-bound (Force of Change)

One desire commonly traces to more than one force. A cordless styling tool's
appeal is Comfort (Mass Instinct) *and* Incompatibility (the Mass Technological
Problem of a cord that doesn't fit the room). Record both — the layering is
what makes an angle feel specific rather than generic.

---

## The ranking criteria

Applied in Section 5 of the deliverable.

**Scope** — how many people share this desire. A desire held intensely by a tiny
group is a niche product, not a mass-market angle. Evidence: how broadly the
language appears across different communities, not how many times it appears in
one.

**Urgency** — how desperately they want relief *now*. Look for time pressure in
the language: "finally," "immediately," "constantly," "I've had it." Urgency is
the difference between a desire someone acts on and one they agree with.

**Staying Power** — does the desire renew continuously or exhaust itself. This is
where the force mapping pays off. Mass Instincts renew forever. Mass
Technological Problems renew until someone in the category solves them. Forces
of Change decay.

A desire strong on all three is rare and should lead the document. A desire
strong on Urgency but weak on Scope is a sub-avatar angle, not a hero angle —
say which it is rather than dropping it.
