# Market sophistication

The canonical reference for the EVOLVE pipeline. `evolve-new-info`,
`evolve-angles`, and `evolve-video-ads` all cite this file.

> Reconstructed from the fragments quoted across the EVOLVE prompts plus Eugene
> Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*, because the original "Market
> Sophistication Document" the prompts refer to was not included in the source
> material. If the team's own version surfaces, it should replace this file.

## What sophistication measures

Not how smart the audience is. **How many times they have already heard a claim
like yours.**

Sophistication is a property of the *market*, not the product or the customer.
Two people with identical problems sit at different stages depending on how much
advertising they have absorbed in that category. It rises over time and never
falls, because a market cannot un-hear a promise.

Getting the stage wrong is the most expensive error in the pipeline. Copy pitched
a stage too low reads as naive and gets ignored. A stage too high reads as
confusing and gets ignored. Same outcome, opposite cause.

## The five stages

### Stage 1 — First to market

Nobody has made this claim before. Simply naming the promise is enough.

> "Stops hair damage."

Rare, and usually mistaken. Most "Stage 1" reads are really Stage 3 markets
where the strategist hasn't looked at competitors.

### Stage 2 — Competitors have arrived

The claim has been made. Now it escalates: bigger, faster, more.

> "Stops hair damage in half the time."

Works until everyone is shouting, which happens quickly.

### Stage 3 — Claims stop being believed → **NEW MECHANISM**

The audience has heard every version of the promise and believed none. Escalating
further makes it worse. What restores belief is a change in **why** — a new
process, technology, ingredient, or delivery system that explains the result.

> "Ceramic plates hold a steady 300°F, so the strand never crosses the damage
> threshold."

The promise is unchanged. The *reason* is new, so the promise becomes credible
again. **This is what `evolve-new-mechanism` produces.**

### Stage 4 — Mechanisms stop being believed → **NEW INFORMATION**

Now even the mechanisms are familiar; every brand has one. What breaks through is
genuinely new knowledge — recent research, a newly identified cause, a discovery
that reframes the problem itself.

> "2024 research found the damage isn't heat at all — it's how fast the strand
> cools afterwards."

The audience isn't being asked to believe a better claim. They're being told
something they didn't know. **This is what `evolve-new-info` produces.**

### Stage 5 — Nothing is believed → reposition

The market is exhausted. The remaining moves are identification (sell to who they
*are*, not what the product does) or finding a new market where the same product
sits at Stage 1 or 2.

> Not "this styles hair" but "for hair that's changed."

## Locating a market

Read the top-spending competitor ads in the category and ask what they're doing:

| What competitors are doing | Stage | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody advertises this at all | 1 | State the promise |
| Everyone states the same promise | 2 | Escalate it |
| Everyone escalates; nobody's believed | 3 | New mechanism |
| Everyone has a mechanism | 4 | New information |
| Every mechanism and discovery is worn out | 5 | Reposition or new market |

Two practical notes.

**Judge from ads, not products.** Sophistication is set by what's been *said*,
which is why a competitive-ad sweep beats a product-feature comparison for
locating the stage.

**Sub-markets differ.** The same product can face a Stage 4 market in its core
audience and Stage 2 in an adjacent one — which is exactly what makes Stage 5
repositioning work. When a market feels exhausted, check whether a neighbouring
audience has heard any of it.

---

## Mechanism and information together

Stage 3 and Stage 4 are not alternatives; they stack. The strongest EVOLVE ads
run new information as the hook (*here is something you didn't know*) and the new
mechanism as the resolution (*and here is the thing that acts on it*).

The Flow Pouches example in the source material does exactly this: the new
information is that digestion destroys most of the active compound; the new
mechanism is buccal absorption that bypasses digestion. Neither half works alone
— the information without the mechanism is a problem with no solution, and the
mechanism without the information is a solution to a problem nobody knew they
had.

## Where each EVOLVE skill sits

| Skill | Stage it serves |
|---|---|
| `evolve-desires` | All — desires are constant; sophistication changes only how you speak to them |
| `evolve-new-mechanism` | 3 |
| `evolve-new-info` | 4 |
| `evolve-angles` | 3–4, translating both into reasons to buy |
| `evolve-video-ads`, `evolve-static-ads` | Execute whatever the stage demands |

Awareness and sophistication are different axes and get confused constantly.
**Awareness** is what one person knows about their problem and your product.
**Sophistication** is what the whole market has already been told. A
problem-aware customer can sit in a Stage 4 market; the ad has to handle both.
