# The frameworks

Lenses for thinking, not templates to complete.

---

## The Big 4 emotions

Every major section should hit **2–3 of these powerfully**. Forcing all four
produces checklist copy.

| Emotion | Communicated through |
|---|---|
| **NEW / ONLY** | Discovery language, "first time", contrarian angles, "viral" |
| **EASY / ANYBODY** | "without trying", "automatically", "just X", "easiest way" |
| **SAFE / PREDICTABLE** | Social proof, "not your fault", scientific validation |
| **BIG / FAST** | Specific timelines and percentages |

**Forced** — "12,000 women used the first supplement with Dyglomera that's easy
to take and risk-free and saw results in 3 days." Reads as a feature list.

**Natural** — "12,000 women blocked the enzyme destroying their appetite hormone
— and ate 24% less in week one without trying." NEW/ONLY in the mechanism,
BIG/FAST in the numbers, EASY implied in "without trying" and never announced.

Ask: *which 2–3 emotions hit hardest for THIS angle?* Lead with those. Let the
rest live in subtext.

## The Four U's — for hooks

Caples' priority order. **Hit at least three strongly.**

**1. UNIQUE** — most important, must be present. Without it nothing else
matters. Novel mechanism, contrarian angle, discovery language, a twist that
stops the pattern.

**2. USEFUL** — connects to a desire they actually have. Their stated problem,
not what you want to tell them.

**3. URGENT** — FOMO and time sensitivity. "happening now", "after 40",
"before it's too late".

**4. ULTRA-SPECIFIC** — the believability multiplier. Numbers ("3x more", "24%",
"12,000 women"), names, timelines ("day 10", "by week one").

UNIQUE must almost always be present — it's the scroll-stopping element. The
other three flex. Different U combinations produce **5–10× differences** in
response rate, which is the entire reason for writing three hooks.

## Hopkins' principles

**The 1–2 second rule.** The hook has 1–2 seconds to stop the scroll. If they
don't stop, nothing else matters.

**The law of focus.** ONE central proposition, hammered throughout. Schlitz Beer
became #1 on "beer from bottles washed with live steam" — every competitor did
it, they just said it first. A script with one message penetrates; a script with
ten dilutes.

**Self-interest.** People are interested in themselves. Not your company, not
your product. Frame every element through *their* lens.

- ❌ "Our company spent 10 years developing this"
- ✅ "For 10 years, you've been told it's your fault. It's not."

**Specificity.** *"Platitudes and generalities roll off the human mind like water
off a duck's back."*

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "Lowest prices" | "Costs 2 cents per pound less" |
| "Very effective" | "Reduces symptoms by 64%" |
| "Works fast" | "Cravings gone by day 10" |
| "Many customers" | "12,000 women" |
| "Expert designed" | "Designed by Harvard-trained neuroscientist Dr. [Name]" |
| "Highest quality" | "Triple-filtered through a 7-stage process" |

**Reason-why.** Every claim needs a reason. Not what it does — *why* it works.

- ❌ "It reduces appetite"
- ✅ "It blocks the enzyme that destroys your appetite hormone every 2 minutes, so your brain finally gets the stop signal"

## The slippery slope

Every sentence creates an open loop pulling to the next. Never give them a
natural place to stop before the CTA.

**End with intrigue**
- ❌ "Regular sheets trap heat. That's why you wake up sweaty."
- ✅ "Regular sheets trap heat. But that's not the real problem…"

**Open loops**
- ❌ "This supplement has three ingredients that work together."
- ✅ "This supplement has three ingredients. The first does something no other supplement can do."

**"But wait" moments**
- ❌ "It blocks the enzyme. That helps with appetite."
- ✅ "It blocks the enzyme destroying your appetite hormone every 2 minutes. Here's why that changes everything…"

**Pattern interrupts**
- ❌ "Most people think dandruff comes from dry scalp. They're wrong."
- ✅ "Most people think dandruff comes from dry scalp. It's actually the opposite…"

Test every transition: *does this sentence make them NEED the next one?* Read the
script aloud — any natural stopping point before the CTA gets rewritten.

## Categorisation = death

**The single non-negotiable rule.** The moment you say what the product is
*like*, it becomes a commodity version of that thing.

| Categorises | Owns a new category |
|---|---|
| "Like Ozempic but natural" | "The first supplement that blocks the enzyme destroying your appetite hormone" |
| "Better than other collagens" | — |
| "The best massage gun" | "The only device that reads your muscle tension and adjusts pressure automatically" |
| "Theragun alternative" | — |
| "Similar to X but with Y" | "The first mattress that adjusts firmness based on your body temperature" |

**The test:** if you named the category — massage gun, collagen, mattress,
curler — you have categorised it. Describe what it **does** that's unique.

**Check before finalising any copy:**

- Did I say what this is "like"?
- Did I compare it to an existing brand or category?
- Did I use generic category language?
- Did I position it as "the first/only thing that [unique mechanism]"?

If you catch yourself categorising: stop and rewrite. This is the fastest way to
become just another commodity.

## Show don't tell

- ❌ TELLING: "You'll have more energy and better focus"
- ✅ SHOWING: "Imagine waking up without hitting snooze three times. Walking past the vending machine at 3pm without even looking. Going to bed without the nightly pantry raid."

In visuals:

- ❌ Animated enzymes and scientific diagrams
- ✅ Woman confidently walking past the vending machine at 3pm, not even glancing

---

## Desire intensification (Schwartz)

You cannot create desire — it already exists. Your job is to make them feel it
more strongly.

1. **IDENTIFY** the mass desire the market already has
2. **INTENSIFY** it through contrast, agitation, validation
3. **CHANNEL** it toward your mechanism

---

## Mechanism naming

Give the mechanism a proprietary name where possible — it makes it feel unique
and ownable, and it survives being retold by a customer.

"The Alpine Ice Hack." "The Dyglomera Effect." "The 5-Second Water Method."
