# Section playbook

Requirements for each of the four sections, plus the visual system.

---

## The visual execution system

All scripts are **B-roll compilation + AI voiceover**. No talking heads, no
direct-to-camera. Avatar-specific footage showing the problem → solution
journey, with text overlays.

### Sourcing

**Stock libraries (primary)** — Gridbank (built for ads), Storyblocks, Pexels.
Source: avatar experiencing the problem (frustrated, struggling), avatar
experiencing the solution (confident, relieved), product shots, lifestyle
context.

> VISUAL: Gridbank footage — 40-year-old woman tossing in bed, frustrated,
> checking clock showing 3am. Text overlay: "Regular sheets trap heat"

**fal.ai (when stock doesn't exist)** — abstract concepts (enzymes, hormones,
mechanisms), microscopic or scientific visuals, impossible-to-film scenarios.

> VISUAL: fal.ai generated — microscopic view of silver-infused fabric threads,
> blue and silver tones, scientific aesthetic, clean background

### Instruction format

`VISUAL: [Source] — [avatar demographic] [action/emotion], [context]. Text overlay: "[text]"`

**Do:** be specific about demographics, describe the emotion or behaviour, include
the text overlay, match the visual to the exact voiceover moment, show
behavioural transformation.

**Don't:** request professional actors or studio shoots, suggest filming anyone,
use vague descriptions ("person looking happy"), forget text overlays — they're
critical for sound-off mobile viewing.

### Pacing

| Section | Pacing |
|---|---|
| Hook 0–3s | Fast cut or one dramatic shot matching hook intensity |
| Bridge 3–8s | 2–3 quick cuts showing frustration |
| Hold 8–35s | Product shots, mechanism visuals, solution scenarios; slow slightly |
| CTA 35–45s | Clean product shot, pricing, guarantee badge, CTA text |

## Section A — Hook (0–3s)

**Goal:** stop the scroll by communicating 2–3 of the Big 4 through subtext, in
1–2 seconds.

### Length is the thing people get wrong

**Capture the idea in ~5 words** (3–7 acceptable). **Maximum 2 lines on mobile.**

Long hooks fail three ways: they feel like work, they scream "ad", and they kill
intrigue by putting the writer in explanatory mode.

Think about the **visual shape**: should look like a headline, scannable in under
a second. Not a paragraph, not 3–4 lines.

- ❌ "If you've been struggling with your weight for years and have tried every diet under the sun but nothing seems to work and you're finally ready to discover the real reason why…" — wall of text, instant scroll
- ✅ "After 40, your body makes 3x more of the enzyme destroying your appetite hormone." — 2 lines, scannable, creates curiosity
- ✅ "Why eating 1,200 calories feels like torture." — 1 line, punchy

### The two frameworks — choose one per hook

**1. "This is the biggest thing happening now"** — scale, urgency, magnitude.
Language like hottest, fastest growing, biggest, viral.
> "After 40, your body makes 3x more of this enzyme"
> "The viral sheets that regulate body temperature"

**2. "This is quirky"** — a small counter-intuitive twist. Doesn't have to
destroy worlds, just needs a quirk.
> "The easiest way to get rid of dandruff"
> "Here's why I stopped using regular sheets"

### Three hooks, three entry points

1. **Discovery-led** from the new mechanism document (NEW/ONLY dominant)
2. **Contrarian / "what they're not telling you"** from new information (NEW/ONLY + SAFE)
3. **Emotional pain validation** from customer data (SAFE + BIG dominant)

### Do / don't

**Do:** ~5 words, max 2 lines, create curiosity, use discovery language
("revealed", "discovered", "scientists found"), lead with the mechanism or
contrarian angle, scannable in under a second.

**Don't:** write 4–5 line hooks, explain the opportunity, list benefits, go into
educational mode, give away the answer.

The hook's job is to stop the scroll and create curiosity — **not** to explain
how everything works. That's what the next 40 seconds are for.

## Section B — Bridge (3–8s)

**Goal:** intensify the desire and transition from hook to mechanism through
objection handling. The Objection → Claim cycle begins here.

- Address the implied objection — skepticism, past failures
- Validate frustration in **exact customer language** from testimonials
- Debunk misconceptions with contrarian angles
- Explain **why previous attempts failed**
- Paint the contrast: current pain vs. desire fulfilled

**Emotional targets — hit 2–3:** SAFE (validate: "it's not your fault"), NEW
(introduce the villain — the enzyme, the hidden cause), BIG (quantify the
problem), EASY (imply the solution exists and is reachable).

Keep it under 5 seconds. Every sentence creates curiosity for the next.

> VISUAL: Gridbank footage — woman frustrated at scale, shaking head. Cut to her
> staring at food, internal struggle visible. Text overlay: "NOT YOUR FAULT"

## Section C — Hold (8–35s)

**Goal:** run the Objection → Claim → Proof → Benefit cycle **once**, cleanly,
while introducing the ONE mechanism that creates new hope.

This is the longest section, but it's still **one cycle**. Multiple cycles create
length and complexity.

**Law of focus applies hardest here.**
- ❌ "It has Dyglomera which boosts GLP-1, plus Suntheanine for cortisol, plus marine collagen for skin, plus…"
- ✅ "Metaglow blocks the enzyme destroying your appetite hormone. That's it. One mechanism. Here's why it works…"

Supporting ingredients can be mentioned, but always tie back to the one central
mechanism.

### The cycle

**1. OBJECTION (8–11s)** — address the doubt directly. *"I know what you're
thinking: I've tried supplements before."* End with a gap: *"But here's what
they didn't tell you…"*

**2. CLAIM (11–16s)** — the unique mechanism, without categorising. *"Metaglow is
the first and only supplement that blocks the enzyme destroying your appetite
hormone."* Every claim needs its reason. End with intrigue.

**3. PROOF (16–25s)** — evidence. Clinical data ("they ate 24% fewer calories in
week one"), mechanism explanation at 6th-grade level, social proof if the volume
is real. Don't just state facts — *"And here's what happened next…"*

**4. BENEFIT (25–32s)** — SHOW the transformation.
- ❌ "You'll have better appetite control"
- ✅ "Imagine eating breakfast and not thinking about lunch at 10am. Walking past the vending machine at 3pm without even looking. Going to bed without raiding the pantry."

**Optional secondary benefit (32–35s)** — one only, under 3 seconds, not a
separate cycle. *"Plus it has marine collagen for skin and joints."*

### Writing rules

Simple spoken language, no jargon, always ask "would someone say this out loud?"
Feature-benefit sandwich: state the delivery method, then the emotional benefit
in customer transformation language. Target 15–20 seconds for the core cycle,
extend to 27 if needed.

## Section D — Offer & CTA (35–45s)

**Goal:** maintain emotional momentum through the close. *New and novel isn't
just for your headline — it applies to your offer too.*

**Structure:** summarise the transformation and close the loop back to the hook →
pricing with value framing → bonus stack if applicable → risk reversal with a
pattern interrupt → urgency through value, not fake scarcity → clear CTA.

**Emotional targets — hit 2–3:**
- **NEW/ONLY** — pattern interrupt in the guarantee, unusual terms
- **EASY** — "$1.57 per day", "just tap below", "starts working in 2 weeks"
- **SAFE** — "60-day money-back guarantee", "keep the first jar anyway"
- **BIG/FAST** — "save 20%", "relief in the next 2 weeks", "less than a coffee"

**Pattern interrupt:**
- ❌ "Try it risk-free"
- ✅ "Here's what's crazy: try it risk-free for 60 days. If it doesn't work, keep the first jar anyway. Why? Because we'd rather lose money than have you think this is another scam supplement."

**Keep the slope to the end:**
- ❌ "Try it now. It's risk-free. Order below."
- ✅ "Your appetite can finally calm down in the next 2 weeks. Try it free for 60 days — if it doesn't work, keep the first jar anyway. Because you're not weak. Your body's been fighting you. Tap below to fix it."

**Loop closure checklist:** callback to the hook, remove the final objection
("you're not weak"), social proof reminder if real, clear action, product name,
fast timeline.

## Product type adaptations

**Health / supplement / high-tech** — emphasise the mechanism in the hold with
specific ingredient names, clinical visual aesthetic, scientific credibility for
SAFE. Never compare to drugs or existing supplements.

**Low-to-mid priced apparel** — pain point and aspiration in hook and bridge,
transformation imagery showing behavioural outcomes, user volume for SAFE if
real, emphasise EASY (fits existing wardrobe and lifestyle).

**High perceived value / luxury** — lead with over-delivery, maximise perceived
bonus value, money-back guarantees and exclusive access for SAFE, focus on BIG
transformation and status outcomes.
