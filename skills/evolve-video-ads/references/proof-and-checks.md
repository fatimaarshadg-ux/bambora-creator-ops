# Social proof, compliance, and the final checks

---

## Social proof scaling

Most brands don't have 10,000 customers. Many are white-label or just starting.
Proof has to work at any scale — and inventing volume is the one thing that
can't be done.

| Customers / reviews | What to say |
|---|---|
| **10,000+** | "Over 10,000 five-star reviews" · "12,000 women trust this" · "Rated 4.8/5 by 15,000+" |
| **1,000–10,000** | "Thousands of five-star reviews" · "Trusted by thousands" · "Rated 4.8/5 by thousands" |
| **100–1,000** | "Hundreds of five-star reviews" · lead with percentage: "94% would recommend" |
| **10–100** | "Rated 4.8/5 stars" · percentages over volume: "87% saw results in week one" · lead with testimonial content |
| **Under 10 / white-label** | Borrowed proof, clinical data, or transformation language — or skip volume entirely and prove the mechanism |

**Percentages scale down gracefully where volume doesn't.** "94% would
recommend" reads as strong at any base; "hundreds of customers" reads as weak.
When volume is thin, switch axes rather than reaching for a bigger number.

## The borrowed proof protocol

For products another company created.

**Allowed:**
- The actual brand's review count or rating, if you're an authorised reseller
- Clinical studies and trials from the manufacturer
- Ingredient research and scientific backing
- Generic testimonial patterns ("customers report…")

**Not allowed:**
- Claiming YOUR company has 10,000 reviews when it doesn't
- Made-up customer names or stories
- Attributing invented stats to your brand

**Example — white-label coffee:**
- ❌ "Our coffee has helped over 100,000 people" (if you just started selling it)
- ✅ "This coffee blend is rated 4.9/5 with over 100,000 reviews" (if true of the actual brand)
- ✅ "Customers report better focus within 30 minutes" (generic pattern, no fabrication)

## Final checks

Run all of these before delivering. The three marked non-negotiable are the ones
that invalidate the whole script.

### Modular output — NON-NEGOTIABLE

- Three different hooks?
- Three different bridges, one per hook?
- **ONE** universal hold — not three?
- **ONE** universal CTA — not three?
- Shown how they combine into three test scripts?
- Hold identical across all three?
- CTA identical across all three?

If you wrote three different holds or CTAs, you did it wrong. Stop and
restructure. Only hooks and bridges vary.

### Hook length and intrigue — NON-NEGOTIABLE

- ~5 words (3–7 range)?
- Maximum 2 lines on mobile?
- Looks like a headline, not a paragraph?
- Creating intrigue rather than explaining?
- Scannable in under 1 second?
- Using one of the two frameworks?
- Creates a curiosity gap?

If a hook is 3+ lines or feels explanatory, it's the wall-of-text trap. Rewrite
to ~5 words that create curiosity instead of explanation.

### Categorisation — NON-NEGOTIABLE

- Did I say what this is "like"?
- Did I compare it to an existing brand?
- Did I use generic category language?
- Did I position as "first/only to [unique mechanism]"?

If you caught yourself categorising: stop, rewrite.

### Compliance

- Are these claims real and based on data actually held?
- If a claim, customer count, or result isn't validated, it must change.
- Using borrowed proof — is it attributed correctly?
- **Are you fabricating social proof numbers?** Don't.

### The Big 4

- Which 2–3 emotions hit hardest for THIS angle — not "did I check all boxes"?
- Does it read naturally or like a feature list?
- Communicating emotion, or stating facts?

### The Four U's

- Which three U's are strongest here?
- Does this stop the scroll or just check boxes?
- Would I personally click this?

### Specificity

Every vague claim replaced with a specific one. *Vague claims are ignored.
Specific claims are believed.*

### Show don't tell

- Showing behavioural transformation, or telling benefits?
- Do visuals depict outcomes (woman walking past the vending machine) rather
  than concepts (animated enzymes)?
- Testimonials showing emotional states rather than listing results?

### Slippery slope

- Does each sentence create curiosity for the next?
- Open loops that resolve later?
- Any stopping points where they could naturally quit?
- Do key sentences end with intrigue rather than resolution?

Read it aloud. Any natural place to stop before the CTA gets rewritten.

### Objection → Claim → Proof → Benefit

- Does the hold address objections before making claims?
- Proof for every claim?
- Benefits shown through behavioural transformation?
- Cycled **once** cleanly, not repeatedly?

### Spoken language

- 5th–7th grade reading level?
- Would someone say this out loud in conversation?
- Contractions and natural speech rhythms?

### Visual execution

- Every instruction specific and actionable?
- Source specified (Gridbank / fal.ai)?
- Avatar demographics and exact scenario described?
- Text overlays included?
- Visuals show transformation, not just product shots?

### Length and structure

- 30–45 seconds, not longer?
- Problem-aware or product-aware only?
- One cycle?
- Slippery slope maintained throughout?

### Human writer check — the most important one

- Does this sound like a human wrote it, or an AI completing a checklist?
- Would a professional copywriter write it this way?
- Natural and flowing, or forced and listy?

If it feels forced: step back, pick the 2–3 strongest emotional levers for the
angle, and let them breathe.

---

## Final thought

> *It's not what you say, it's what you communicate.*

Every word should serve the subtext of emotions that drive action. The reader
doesn't care about checked boxes — they care whether this is NEW, whether they
can EASILY do it, whether it's SAFE to try, and whether it'll make a BIG
difference FAST.

Sometimes hitting two of those powerfully beats forcing all four. The hook
especially must be concise: ~5 words that create intrigue, not 4–5 lines that
feel like work.

And never categorise. Ever.
